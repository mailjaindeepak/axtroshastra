♃Jupiter · Pisces · own sign · combust

  • A planet in a sign it owns is said to be at full strength: nobody's guest, nothing borrowed. In this birth chart, Jupiter is the only graha with that status.

☾Moon · Sagittarius · Purva Ashadha

The nakshatra called “the undefeated”

  • His Moon sign — his rashi — is Sagittarius, and inside it the Moon occupies the nakshatra Purva Ashadha.
  • Classical texts gloss that nakshatra as aparajita: the unconquered, the invincible, the one not defeated. Its symbol is a hand-fan, its ruling planet Venus, and its traditional themes are unshakeable conviction and refusal to be talked down.
  • That is what the tradition says about the placement. It is a description of an old symbol, not a claim about a fifteen-year-old's character.
🔭 Moon in Sagittarius, Purva Ashadha nakshatra. Holds for all but the last 95 minutes of his birth date — Lesson 05 gives the exact boundary.
☿Mercury · Pisces · debilitated

Not everything in this chart is easy

  • Mercury governs speech, calculation and quick thinking. Pisces is its debilitation sign — the one place in the zodiac where classical rules read it as working with the handbrake on.
  • Mars, also in Pisces, is combust alongside Jupiter. So of the four grahas crowded into that one sign, three carry some classical qualifier.
  • Charts are not scorecards. A debilitated planet is a described condition, not a verdict on a person — and this page is not about to hand a teenager a list of his supposed weak points.
🔭 Mercury in Pisces — debilitated. The counterweight to Jupiter in the same sign.
♄Saturn · Virgo · retrograde

The slow planet, moving backwards

  • Saturn is the graha of patience, structure and the unglamorous long haul. On his birth date it sat in Virgo, and it was retrograde — apparently travelling backwards against the stars, as seen from Earth.
  • Retrograde motion is an optical effect of two planets orbiting at different speeds, not a malfunction. Jyotish reads a retrograde graha as one whose work turns inward and repeats itself rather than proceeding in a straight line.
  • It is the only retrograde graha in the chart apart from the lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu, which are always reckoned retrograde.

LESSON 01

What a birth chart even is

A kundli, or janam kundli, is a snapshot of the sky from the exact spot and second someone was born. Where each planet was sitting, frozen.

Vedic astrology treats that snapshot as the raw material a person was handed — not their fate, their starting kit.

His: 27 March 2011, Samastipur, in Bihar. That sky had four grahas bunched into Pisces — Sun, Mercury, Jupiter and Mars — with Venus in Aquarius, the Moon and Rahu in Sagittarius, Saturn in Virgo and Ketu in Gemini.

LESSON 03

Why “which sign” actually matters

Every planet has signs where it's comfortable and signs where it struggles. Same planet, different room, different output.

  • Home ground (own sign) — the planet owns the place. Full strength, no permission needed.
  • Debilitated (weakest spot) — it's working with the handbrake on.
  • Everywhere else (neutral) — a guest. Functional, not special.
His scorecard: one planet on home ground (Jupiter in Pisces), one debilitated (Mercury, also in Pisces), and the other seven neutral. Both of the non-neutral ones landed in the same sign, which is part of what makes this chart unusual to look at.
LESSON 04

Combust — the asterisk on Jupiter

A planet sitting very close to the Sun in the sky is called combust, or asta. Literally: burnt up. Practically, it is the planet you cannot see because the Sun's glare has swallowed it.

Classical Jyotish reads combustion as a loss of visibility and independent voice — the planet still holds whatever dignity its sign gives it, but has to work through the Sun rather than around it.

In his kundli both Jupiter and Mars are combust, and Jupiter is the interesting one: it has the best possible sign placement and the glare sitting on top of it at the same time. Any page telling you his Jupiter is simply "very strong" has quietly dropped half the sentence.
LESSON 05

Nakshatras — the 27 lunar mansions

The twelve signs are the coarse grid. Underneath them sits a finer one: 27 nakshatras, each a little over 13 degrees wide, each with its own symbol, ruling planet and body of traditional meaning. Which nakshatra the Moon occupies at birth is one of the most-used facts in the whole system.

His Moon sits in Purva Ashadha, ruled by Venus, symbolised by a hand-fan, and traditionally called aparajita — the undefeated.

The honest caveat, since we computed it: the Moon spent almost all of 27 March 2011 crossing Purva Ashadha, and moved into the next nakshatra, Uttara Ashadha, at roughly 10:26 pm India time. So Purva Ashadha is right for any birth in the first twenty-two and a half hours of that day — but a late-night birth would change it. Nobody has published a time either way, so we say so rather than smooth it over.
LESSON 06

Dashas — and why this page gives you no dates

Vedic astrology's timing system, the Vimshottari dasha, hands each planet a turn at running a life, for years at a stretch, in a fixed order. The Moon's nakshatra at birth sets which planet goes first, and how much of that first turn is already used up at the moment of birth.

That second part is the problem. The Moon moves about 13 degrees a day and a nakshatra is only 13 degrees and 20 minutes wide — so across a single day the Moon crosses nearly the whole mansion, and the "how much is already used up" figure swings wildly.

We measured it for his birth date. Sweeping every hour of 27 March 2011: a birth just after midnight puts the end of his first mahadasha in December 2028; a birth at noon puts it in May 2019; a birth at six in the evening puts it in September 2014. That is a fourteen-year spread from one unknown hour. Even the question "which mahadasha is he in right now" has three different answers depending on the minute.

So we publish the order, which the nakshatra does fix — Venus → Sun → Moon → Mars → Rahu → Jupiter → Saturn → Mercury → Ketu — and no dates at all. Any site showing you his dasha timeline to the day has silently picked a birth time and isn't telling you which.
LESSON 07

What we genuinely can't tell you

Here's where most celebrity astrology sites quietly cheat, and we won't.

A chart has two halves. Which sign each planet is in — that only needs the date, so everything above is solid. And the houses, which need the exact minute of birth, because the rising sign changes roughly every two hours.

No birth time has ever been published for him. On his birth date the rising sign moved through all twelve signs, so a houses-based reading would be a guess with twelve possible answers.

So on this page you will not see a lagna, a house, a dasha date, or a manglik verdict. Other sites will publish all four for him. Ask them which birth minute they used, and where they got it.
LESSON 08

Why there are no predictions on this page

One of the things people search for around this name is a future prediction. We're not going to write one, and it's worth saying why rather than just leaving a gap.

He is fifteen. Publishing astrological forecasts about a named child's career, character or personal life is something we won't do at any level of confidence — it isn't ours to write, and a search engine ranking isn't a good enough reason.

What this page does instead: it computes his chart accurately, explains what the placements traditionally mean, celebrates a genuinely remarkable cricketing record, and stops there. If you want a forecast, the honest place to get one is your own chart — not a child's.
